Outcome Briefing

GOLD RESERVES.

Gold is a custody map as much as a tonnage table.

GOLD BRIEF\n> reported tonnes\n> official owner\n> custody route\n> audit route\n> missing records

Evidence Grade

Official reserve data plus source-route verification needed. Reported tonnes do not automatically prove exact storage location.

Core Conclusion

Gold reserve tracking must separate three things: how much a country reports, who officially owns it, and where the metal is held. The gap between reported ownership and public custody detail is the intelligence value.

Mechanism To Track

Reported Holdings

Use official reserve data to show tonnes and gold share of national reserves.

Custody Route

Record whether storage is disclosed domestically, abroad, through a central bank, through a mint, or left unclear.

Audit Route

Track whether there is an inventory statement, annual report, independent audit, internal check or only an aggregate number.

Movement Route

Track public reserve increases, transfers, repatriation statements and changes in reserve share.

Likely Outcomes

  • Gold remains a reserve-power signal during debt, sanctions and currency stress.
  • Central-bank buying remains important as states diversify reserves.
  • Vault and custody transparency becomes more important when trust falls.
  • Repatriation announcements become signal events.
  • Audit disputes become public-pressure events.

Records Needed

  • Central bank annual reports.
  • Official reserve statements.
  • Custody or storage disclosures.
  • Inventory or audit reports.
  • Repatriation records.
  • Treasury or parliamentary statements.

Counter-Points

Reported tonnes are still useful. Some storage secrecy is legitimate for security. Foreign storage can be practical. The briefing upgrades only when records show custody gaps, audit gaps or major reserve changes.

Probability Triggers

  • Reserve signal rises when a country sharply increases reported gold share.
  • Custody concern rises when public audit access is weak.
  • Geopolitical signal rises when repatriation is announced.
  • Concern falls when detailed audits and custody statements are published.
